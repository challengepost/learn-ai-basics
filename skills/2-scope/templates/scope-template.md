# [Project Name]

One line: what this is.

## The Unique Kernel
The one distinctive thing that makes this not generic. State it in a sentence or two.
If you could delete this and nobody would notice the difference, it isn't the kernel — keep digging.

## Who It's For
A specific person, specific enough to picture. Not "people" or "users."
What they're trying to do, and what they do today instead.

## The Core Loop
The one thing the user does over and over, described concretely: they open it, then what?
Why they come back.

## Inspiration & Identity
References, aesthetic, mood, energy, tone — what it should feel like to use.
Include URLs for anything referenced; later phases use these as a research map.
Capture volunteered direction here; don't add a design questionnaire during scope.
`3-prd` fills relevant gaps in 1–2 questions under `prd.md > Look and Feel`;
`4-spec` carries that into `spec.md > Look and Feel`, which guides visible build work.

## Why This Matters to the Learner
The personal stake — in their own words where possible.

## What "Working" Looks Like
Concrete description of the completed proof of concept.
Include the moment that demonstrates it compellingly — the "oh, that's cool" beat.

## The POC Boundary
What is in, stated tightly. If it isn't needed to prove the kernel and the core loop, it isn't in.

## Later
Worth doing, just not now.

## Explicitly Cut
Named, each with a real reason. Nothing disappears silently.
